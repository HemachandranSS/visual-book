UPSC Syllabus

Prelims screens. Mains decides. The interview is the last 275 marks. Prelims marks do not enter the final merit.

Stages Pattern Prelims Mains Interview Optional FAQ GS Maps Prelims PYQ Mains Questions

Stage 1

Prelims

Two objective papers. GS counts for the cutoff. CSAT is qualifying at 33%.

Stage 2

Mains

Nine written papers. Two are qualifying. Seven carry 1750 marks.

Stage 3

Interview

Personality test of 275 marks. Final merit is Mains plus interview.

Exam pattern

Prelims

Paper Questions Marks Negative
GS Paper I, 2 hours 100 200 1/3 of 2
CSAT Paper II, 2 hours 80 200 1/3 of 2.5

CSAT needs at least 33% of 200. Missing either paper disqualifies the attempt. Appearing in even one paper counts as an attempt.

Paper Marks

English, qualifying Paper B300

Each written paper is 3 hours. Qualifying papers need 25% before the rest are evaluated. Paper A is a language from the Eighth Schedule. Optional papers are at honours level, or bachelor’s level for Engineering, Medical Science, and Law.

Prelims syllabus

General Studies

Seven buckets. Current affairs sits on top of every other bucket.

  1. Current events of national and international importance.
  2. History of India and Indian National Movement.
  3. Indian and World Geography — physical, social, and economic geography of India and the world.
  4. Indian Polity and Governance — Constitution, political system, Panchayati Raj, public policy, rights issues.
  5. Economic and Social Development — sustainable development, poverty, inclusion, demographics, social sector initiatives.
  6. General issues on environmental ecology, biodiversity, and climate change that do not require subject specialisation.
  7. General Science.

Mains syllabus

GS I

Indian heritage and culture, history, and geography of the world and society. 250 marks. Open the Geography, Art and Culture, History, Post-Independence, and other GS topic maps.

  1. Indian culture: salient aspects of art forms, literature, and architecture from ancient to modern times.
  2. Modern Indian history from about the middle of the eighteenth century until the present: significant events, personalities, issues.
  3. The freedom struggle: its stages and important contributors from different parts of the country.
  4. Post-independence consolidation and reorganisation within the country.
  5. World history from the 18th century: Industrial Revolution, world wars, redrawing of national boundaries, colonisation, decolonisation, and political philosophies such as communism, capitalism, and socialism, and their effect on society.
  6. Salient features of Indian society, and diversity of India.
  7. Role of women and women’s organisations, population and associated issues, poverty and developmental issues, urbanisation, their problems and remedies.
  8. Effects of globalisation on Indian society.
  9. Social empowerment, communalism, regionalism, and secularism.
  10. Salient features of the world’s physical geography.
  11. Distribution of key natural resources across the world, including South Asia and the Indian subcontinent, and factors behind the location of primary, secondary, and tertiary industries, including in India.
  12. Important geophysical phenomena such as earthquakes, tsunami, volcanic activity, and cyclones; changes in critical geographical features, including water bodies and ice caps, and in flora and fauna, and the effects of such changes.

Exam notes

1. What is the eligibility criteria for the Civil Services Examination (CSE) 2026?
Nationality:Must be a citizen of India for IAS, IFS, and IPS. For other services, candidates may also be a subject of Nepal, Bhutan, a Tibetan refugee, or an individual of Indian origin from specified countries.
Age Limit:Candidates must be between 21 and 32 years and the cutoff date to calculate age is August 1. Relaxations apply for certain categories.
Educational Qualification:Must hold a graduate degree or equivalent from a recognized institution.
2. How many attempts are allowed for the Civil Services Examination?
General Category:6 attempts.
SC/ST Category:Unlimited attempts.
OBC Category:9 attempts.
PwBD Category:9 attempts for General, EWS, and OBC categories; unlimited for SC/ST.
3. What are the key stages in the Civil Services Examination?
Preliminary Examination:Objective type with two papers (General Studies and CSAT).
Main Examination:Written examination with 9 papers, including compulsory and optional papers.
Interview/Personality Test:For those who qualify the Main Examination.
4. What is the fee structure for the Civil Services Examination?
General, OBC, and EWS categories:Rs. 100.
Women, SC/ST, PwBD candidates:Exempt from the fee.
5. What documents are required to apply for the CSE?
Identity Proof:Valid photo ID (Aadhar, PAN, Passport, etc.).
Educational Certificates:Proof of graduation or final year mark sheet.
Caste/Category Certificate:For candidates claiming reservation benefits.
Photograph and Signature:Scanned copies in prescribed format (JPEG, PNG and PDF).
6. Can candidates change their category or service preference after submitting the application?
Category Change:Requests for category changes (e.g., from OBC to SC/ST) will not be entertained.
Service Preference:Candidates must carefully select their service preferences during application, and once submitted, these cannot be changed.
7. How are candidates selected for the Civil Services (Main) Examination?
Candidates are selected based on their performance in the Preliminary Examination. Only those who qualify the Preliminary Exam will be admitted to the Main Examination.
8. How many candidates are selected for the Mains Examination?
The number of candidates selected in the Preliminary Examination to appear in the Civil Services Main Examination is typically between twelve and thirteen times the total number of vacancies to be filled.
  • This means that if there are 'X' vacancies, approximately 12X to 13X candidates will be admitted to the Mains examination.
9. How many candidates are selected for the Interview?
The number of candidates selected in the Mains Examination to appear for the Civil Services Interview/Personality Test is typically 2.5 to 3 times the total number of vacancies to be filled.
  • This means that if there are 'X' vacancies, approximately 2.5X to 3X candidates will be shortlisted for the Interview stage.
10. Is there any relaxation in age or attempts for differently-abled candidates?
Yes, candidates with benchmark disabilities (PwBD) are provided relaxation in age limits and the number of attempts, depending on their category.
11. How do I apply for the Civil Services Examination?
Candidates need to apply online through the UPSC website. They must complete a four-stage application process, including registration, filling out personal and academic details, and paying the application fee.
12. Will the marks obtained in the Preliminary Examination be counted for the final result?
No, the marks obtained in the Preliminary Examination will not be considered when determining the final merit order of the candidates.
13. Is it mandatory to appear in both papers of the Civil Services (Preliminary) Examination?
Yes, candidates must appear in both papers of the Preliminary Examination for the purpose of evaluation. Failure to appear in both will result in disqualification.
14. If I appear in only one Paper in Prelims Examination, will it be counted as an attempt?
Yes, if a candidate appears in any one paper of the Preliminary Examination, it will count as an attempt.
15. What happens if a candidate fails to appear in one of the Preliminary Examination papers?
If a candidate does not appear in both papers of the Civil Services (Preliminary) Examination, they will be disqualified from the evaluation process.
16. How many candidates are typically selected for the Civil Services (Main) Examination?
Approximately 12 to 13 times the number of vacancies are selected from the candidates who qualify in the Civil Services (Preliminary) Examination to appear for the Civil Services (Main) Examination.
17. Is there any provision for candidates who reach the final interview stage but are not recommended?
Yes, non-recommended candidates at the interview stage can consent to share their details with registered organizations (Public and Private Sector) for potential employment opportunities.
18. Which categories of disabilities are eligible for reservation under PwBD?
Eligible Disabilities:
  • Blindness and low vision
  • Deaf and hard of hearing
  • Locomotor disabilities (including cerebral palsy, leprosy cured, dwarfism, acid attack victims, muscular dystrophy)
  • Multiple disabilities

These papers are qualifying in nature. Candidates must score at least 25% in each to have their other papers evaluated.
20. Can candidates with disabilities avail reservation?
Yes, candidates with disabilities (PwBD) can avail of the reservation. The eligibility for reservation will depend on the nature of the disability, and candidates must meet the specific requirements outlined in the notice.
21. Can the chosen examination center be changed after submission of the form?
No requests for changes in the examination center will be entertained after submission of the form.
